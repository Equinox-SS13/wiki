# Character Information
Your sprite isn't the end-all-be-all of how your character looks. Your character's flavor text and pose are also ways to communicate who they are. 

Your character's look isn't the only thing that people will see about them. Colony administration is going to want a record of any security incidents or concerns. Medical might want a medical history. And the department you're working in might have your resume on file. These things are called character records. 

This guide will go over how to write good flavor text, a pose, and how to write your character's records.

# Flavor Text and Pose
Two ways to show your character off are flavor text and pose. Flavor text is a more permanent description of your character. Pose is temporary, and only lasts until you clear it or the round ends.

## Flavor Text
Flavor text is a short contemporaneous sensory description of who your character is. To put it simply, if you can't hear, see, feel, or smell it from standing in front of them for a little bit, don't put it in there. Examples of things to include are height, build, skin/fur/scale/etc. color, and eye color. You can also put in common mannerisms that they might exhibit while standing still. Ear flicks, tail swishes, tongue flicking, picking their teeth, etc. are all good examples. 

Here is a bad example of flavor text. 
> [CHARACTER] is a tall wolf with brown eyes and grey fur. He thinks he's better than you, and his time in the Port Authority shows it, with over three hundred confirmed kills.

The reason that this is bad isn't because it references a "certified badass." It's bad because you wouldn't be able to tell this by looking at him. Here's how to write it a little better:
> [CHARACTER] is a tall wolf with brown eyes and grey fur. He can usually be caught with a sneer on his face, or a derisive look in his eye. A set of patches on his jacket denote exceptional service in the Port Authority.

By changing the things you can't tell to things you can, it promotes asking questions, making assumptions, or generally interacting with the character. When in doubt, follow the idea of "Show, don't tell." This can manifest in, for example, your character showing their attitude through facial expressions, showing they're hyperactive by bouncing all the time or tapping their foot, and showing that they work long hours or are tired by having bags under their eyes. 

## Pose
Pose is a temporary flavor text accessed in-game by using the Set-Pose verb (typed into your chatbox) or clicking on the Set Pose option in the IC tab in the top right of your screen. This is good for temporary poses, mannerisms, or similar. It shows on examine in the format: "He/She is ..." with the ... replaced with the text you input intothe verb. Remember, show, don't tell here. Just like flavor text, it should show what's going on with your character instead of telling what they're going through.

A bad pose example:
> He is biting his fingernails because he's nervous about being shot.

Rewriting this into a good pose example is simple. 
> He is biting his fingernails and eyeing the nearby gun nervously.


# Records
Character records are an administrative view of your character's history. Generally, the higher up in the chain of command you are, the more employment records you should have. 

There are three kinds of records: Medical, Security, and Employment.

## Medical Records

Medical records contain medical information about your character. Allergies, prescriptions, past physicals and psych evals, and similar are all contained in this one document. You can either make up doctors or, with permission, ask someone who has a medical character to be included in the records themselves as the physician performing the exam. One way to get medical roleplay is to ask for a records update in-game!

## Security Records

Security records contain, basically, your rap sheet. Anything that involved security or police in your character's past, or things that security/police should know of, are contained in these records. Most characters should have a relatively blank security record, but past troublemakers or people trying to work off a troubled past might have more! A record should, however, list basic identifying information like name and species. It's totally okay to put "No incidents of note" under your character's basic identifying information if your character doesn't have anything else. 

Here is an example of how to format a security record:

Race: !!!RACEHERE!!!

!!!OFFENSEHERE!!!/!!!NOTES!!!/!!!PLACEOFORIGIN!!!/!!!DATE!!!
!!!OFFENSEHERE!!!/!!!NOTES!!!/!!!PLACEOFORIGIN!!!/!!!DATE!!!

Follow the same instructions as the medical records! If you've been involved with security repeatedly, go ahead and copy the !!!OFFENSEHERE!!! line as many times as you need.

## Employment Records
Employment records are like your resume. List qualifications, duration of employment, and employer to fill out your character's work history. A longer work history is a good way to justify a character being in a more important or secure position, such as a head of staff. You don't have to write a lot, just a little bit of basics. Years, basic qualifications, and a generic employer work well.

Here is an example of how to format an employment record:
> [hr][hr][center][h1][u]Active Contract Details[/u][/h1][/center]
[center][small][i]Provided to the Colony[/i][/small][/center][hr][hr][small]Name: !!!YOURNAMEHERE!!!
Start of Contract: !!!INITIALDATEHERE!!!
Length of Contract: !!!ENDDATEHERE!!!
[br]Company: !!!COMPANYNAMEHERE!!!
Department: !!!DEPARTMENTHERE!!!
Position: !!!POSITIONHERE!!!
Qualifications: !!!QUALSHERE!!!
[br]Notes: !!!FLUFFHERE!!![hr][hr][small][i]All your paperwork needs, in short and simple formatting![/i][/small][hr][hr]

Just like the above two records, remember to fill out the information. If your character has had multiple jobs, copy the block from Start of Contract to Qualifications, and input all that information for each job. 

# Exploitable Information
There's one last piece of records: Exploitable information. Exploitable information is information only viewable to antagonists, unlike other records which can be viewed by the various departments that should have access to them. Exploitable information can be things like secret debts, shameful secrets, ways to emotionally needle your character, and similar. These could be as simple as a debt you haven't repaid or as complex as the location of the family you've been hiding from antagonists for decades. Pick something that provides for interesting storytelling, or add multiple!
